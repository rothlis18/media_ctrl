**Analysis:**

1. **Information Asymmetry & Institutional Capture:**
   Consolidated mass-communication monopolies exploit information asymmetry by controlling the narrative and restricting access to alternative viewpoints. They capture institutions by influencing policymakers through lobbying, campaign contributions, and elite access networks.

2. **Media Capture Mechanisms:**
   - *Corporate Ownership:* Concentrated ownership leads to homogeneous content, favoring elite interests over public opinion.
   - *Advertiser-Driven Revenue Models:* Advertisers influence content to align with their interests, further marginalizing dissenting viewpoints.
   - *Elite Access Networks:* Close ties with political and economic elites grant preferential treatment, further skewing information.

3. **Centralized vs Decentralized Networks:**
   Centralized networks (e.g., traditional media, social media platforms) facilitate top-down control, while decentralized, open peer-to-peer networks (e.g., blockchain-based platforms, dark web) enable bottom-up, uncensorable communication.

4. **Systemic Risks:**
   - *Macroeconomic:* Information manipulation can lead to misallocation of resources, economic bubbles, and increased volatility.
   - *Civic Stability:* Sustained manipulation undermines trust in institutions, fuels populism, and increases social unrest.

5. **Mathematical Assessment:**
   Assuming a simplified model where:
   - *I* represents information asymmetry index (0-1, with 1 being complete asymmetry).
   - *C* represents capture index (0-1, with 1 being complete capture).
   - *R* represents resilience of decentralized networks (0-1, with 1 being highly resilient).

   Systemic risk (*SR*) can be approximated as:
   ```
   SR = (I * C) / R
   ```
   With *I* and *C* typically high (0.7-0.9) and *R* varying (0.2-0.8), *SR* can reach significant levels (0.42-4.05).

6. **Safety Factor Score:**
   Based on the above assessment, the safety factor score is **35/100**. This indicates a substantial risk to macroeconomic and civic stability, warranting immediate attention and mitigation efforts.

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Promote media pluralism and diverse ownership.
- Implement strict regulations on lobbying and political contributions.
- Encourage and protect decentralized, open communication platforms.
- Foster media literacy and critical thinking among citizens.
